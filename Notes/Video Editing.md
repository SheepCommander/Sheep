10 types of cuts https://youtu.be/YtULPT1aBWM
1. The cut
2. Jump cut (not continuity error, but something that shows the passing of time)
3. Match cut (match action, shape, color, framing, or even audio ~ just a seamless transition)
	1. Graphic match cut (visual elements matching)
	2. Movement match cut (camera, character, or object matching)
	3. Audio match cut (sound matches between two scenes)
4. J-Cut (when the audio from the next scene starts before it's shown)
5. L-Cut (when the audio from the last scene is still being played)
	1. Both good for dialogue
6. The Fade (when a shot dissolves or fades two or from a solid color, usually black. White would be a character dying or in a dream.)
7. The Dissolve (fade to a new shot. To show a passing of time within a scene, or from one scene to the next.)
8. Cutting on Action (Cut during character/object's movement. End/"Out" of shot A matches Beginning/"In" point of shot B, it creates a seamless transition.)
9. Cross Cutting (When the editor is cutting back and forth between multiple scenes. Common in Thrillers, or two characters on the phone talking to each other. Tell two stories at the same time.)
10. Smash Cut (harsh, abrupt cut, with contrasting visuals and audio. Example: Loud violent scene cuts to soft silent one.)

1. Focal points (often, wide, minimal shots with only one focal point. Or, busy frames with one dominant subject/focal point. Often used with Rule of Thirds.)
	1. Golden Triangle also works.
2. Leading Lines. (stairways, bridges, halls, ...)
	1. Breaking the rule using Chaotic Lines (like triangle stairs) can convey a sense of chaos or confusion
3. Negative Space (area around subject) Positive space (area given to subject)
	1. Subject larger in frame can show importance.
	2. Extremely wide shots can represent isolation or vulnerability.
	3. Extreme close-ups can show their power, and emotions.
4. Head Room (amount of space above a subject's head - usually kept to a minimum.)
5. Looking Room (or Looking room) is the amount of negative space in front of a subject - usually a lot more than the negative space behind.
	1. Place subject's eyes on the rule of thirds for perfect head & lead room.
	2. Large amounts of head room can convey uncomfortable, small or insignificant. Small amounts of lead room can convey lost, trapped, or isolated.
6. BALANCE - symmetry or weight...
7. Framing - direct focus. A frame within a frame can create a sense of separation, or depth.
8. Depth - foreground, midground, background. - Use Focus. **Make sure to separate subject from background** - even in small rooms have subject as far away from wall as possible, or shoot into a corner.
