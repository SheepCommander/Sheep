1. **Cutting Pronoun + Filter word** (I saw, he realized, She heard, They noticed).
	- These create distance/filters. To start sentences with pronouns without using filter words, change subject:
	- He moved his hand to the table -> His hand moved to the table
	- He sat down on the chair with a squeak -> The chair squeaked under his weight
	- I saw the woman join him at the table -> the woman joined him at the table.
	- I realized they must have known each other already. --> Had they met before?
	1. Take paragraph: Highlight every filter word: Saw, heard, realized, noticed, thought, felt, seemed; and cut at least half of them out.
2. **Vary your sentence length**!! Short = Impactful; Long = slow down/atmosphere.
	1. It's a heartbeat, and so so important. NO MORE SAFE MID LENGTH SENTENCES, FRIEND! MAKE THEM LONG. LONGER.
3. **Vary your sentence content**. Not just what character do does did, but what they're sensing, noticing, remembering, questioning. 
	1. Sensory description (*Looks, smells, feels.*)
	2. Direct character thoughts or commentary
	3. Rhetorical questions
	4. Exclamations or sound effects
	5. Contextual backstory
	6. ... and actions
4. **Passive sentences** feel incomplete - if you're not trying to create a mystery or immediate question, use active tense.
	1. The door was opened to show Millie standing on the other side. --> The door **opens** to Millie standing on the other side.
	2. "There is/are There was/were" - Not necessarily grammatically passive, but still weigh down prose.
	- "There was a chair positioned under the door knob." --> A chair blocked the door. 
	- "There were candles placed along the corridor."  --> Candles flickered along the corridor.
	- Locate subject, place at beginning, use clear and evocative action verb (blocked, flickered).
5. Stop **relying on adverbs**, use stronger verbs.
	1. "He walked angrily into the room." He stomped into the room. "She cried loudly." She sobbed.
	2. Be more original with diction!
	3. Personify objects! "The floorboards groaned under her feet. Vines hugged the walls. Shadows stretched across the floor. Trees loomed over him."
	4. Don't do this in every single line. That becomes overdone. Adverbs aren't the enemy either, just cut out redundant, repetitive, or weakening ones.
	5. **Open page and highlight three adverb sentences.** (whispered softly, ran quickly, smiled happily). Rewrite each one with a single, intentional verb that captures the mood.  (Know when a plain verb will do the trick!)
6. Be specific instead of **vague**. (Eventually, after some time, somehow, some way, practically, literally, kind of, sort of).
	1. These can be enough, but you might have the opportunity to create a sharper image.
	2. "After some time he left the building." --> Marco checked his plastic wristwatch --- three minutes past noon --- before slipping out through the stairwell.
	3. "She somehow found her way to the river." --> She followed the sound of rushing water until the trees opened up to the river bank.
	4. Cut out the fluff!! You don't need to have a time moving word every single time, highlight them and ask yourself: Do I need to replace this with a clear image, or can I just cut this and move on? 
7. **Overusing** participals (-ing verbs). Sparingly, not samey floaty prose. These only work when two things are happening at the same time.
	1. "He pulled out his phone, scrolling through the messages." Technically works since you can scroll while pulling it out at the same time.
	2. "He unlocked the door, walking into the room." ==Doesn't work because you can't unlock and walk into the room at the same time; actions don't overlap.==
	3. Dialogue tags: *"I'm fine," he said, shrugging. "Don't go," she asked, reaching for his sleeve.* These are clunky because the action is dangling off the tag. *He shrugged. "I'm fine." "Don't go." She reached for his sleeve.*
	4. Exactly right choice: when action is literally simultaneous, or when cadence feels right. 
	5. Pick scene and highlight every -ing verb. *Are these happening at the same time? Or am I just stacking actions.* Try rewriting at least half of these into clean past or present tense, or action beats.
